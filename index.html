How organizations get research done

Four models for research capability

Organizations access UX research capability in different ways depending on their needs, their maturity, and their budget. Understanding these models helps you make better decisions about research investment.

Embedded staffing

A researcher joins your team much like an internal hire — attending standups, working in your tools, embedded in your product rhythm. The most integrated model; generally structured hourly or as a contract engagement.

Learn about embedded research

Fractional research

Reserved capacity at a defined number of hours or days per week or month. Effective when research needs are real but don't justify a full-time placement. Clear hours, fixed availability, genuine team integration.

Learn about fractional research

Managed research projects

A research agency owns the project — from planning through synthesis and delivery. Good for organizations without internal capacity, teams that need an outside perspective, or high-stakes research that needs real ownership.

Learn about research agencies

Direct hire recruiting

Building permanent internal research capacity. A different hiring challenge than most — research work is often confidential, portfolios are thin, and vetting depends on professional history and methodological conversations.

Learn about hiring researchers

What good research practice looks like

Methodological range, independence, strong facilitation and synthesis, the ability to work with or create research infrastructure, and research reasoning that connects evidence to product decisions.

AI fluency is a baseline, not a differentiator

Strong researchers today use AI tools for transcription, synthesis support, and documentation — and invest the time saved into doing the human parts more thoroughly. The differentiation is still in facilitation quality, synthesis depth, and organizational impact.
